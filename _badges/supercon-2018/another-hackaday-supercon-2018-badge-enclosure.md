Patrick (Mechatroniiks on Hackaday.io) designed this two-piece 3D-printed enclosure as an aftermarket upgrade for the official Hackaday Supercon 2018 conference badge. It replaces the badge's stock laser-cut acrylic back panel with a printed top and bottom shell, adding a proper rocker power switch and fuller protection for the PCB. Patrick recommends printing it in a durable plastic such as nylon or ASA, assembled with M3 heat-set inserts and flanged screws.

The project was published as a free design rather than sold as a product: Patrick posted STL and STEP files for both enclosure halves, plus a STEP file of the Supercon badge and prototype PCBAs for reference, so any badge owner with access to a 3D printer could make their own. At the time of the last project update, Patrick was still working out the cleanest way to wire the added power switch given the badge board's existing layout.

## Make your own

Files are on the project's Hackaday.io page: `Badge Top Cover.stl`/`.stp` and `Badge Bottom Cover.stl`/`.stp`. To build one: remove the Supercon 2018 badge's original acrylic back panel, print the top and bottom covers, install M3 heat-set inserts, mount a ZF/Cherry-style rocker switch in the cutout, wire it into the badge's power path, and close the shells with M3 flanged button-head screws.
