Tina Belmont designed this 3D-printed enclosure for the badge handed out at the 2018 Hackaday Superconference (the same badge design also used at Hackaday Belgrade that year), working with Voja Antonic, Dusan Petrovic, and a contributor known as Roger. The project page documents an iterative, deadline-driven build: early drafts tested key spacing and screen bezel clearance (revised to 4.5mm), and the finished case was assembled and used at the conference itself.

The enclosure is a two-piece plastic shell printed inside-down for a smooth exterior finish, held together with 1/2" #4 screws. It integrates a full keyboard (letters and numbers) and a D-pad molded or printed as separate key sets in recessed and raised-letter variants, plus a friction-fit cover for the badge's expansion connector and a removable back panel so people could prototype their own expansion boards without redesigning the whole case.

Rather than being sold or distributed as a physical product, the design was shared openly: the Hackaday.io project page hosts ten files, including STL files for the front and back shells, the keyboard key-set archives, and a STEP file of the populated badge board so others could adapt the enclosure to their own designs.
