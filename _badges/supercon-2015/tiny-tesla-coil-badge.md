At the first Hackaday SuperConference in November 2015, attendees were handed a bare piece of copper-clad FR4 as their conference badge and invited to build on it during a badge-hacking contest, with prizes for best deadbug, best blinky, and most over-the-top. Radu Motisan and Sprite_tm (Jeroen Domburg) worked together on two high-voltage badge hacks that day: a voltage-multiplier "spark gap" badge (which won Best Deadbug and has its own archive entry) and a second, more ambitious attempt to build an actual Tesla coil onto a badge.

The Tesla coil badge never got that far. Motisan wound a secondary coil from a length of orange Kynar wire salvaged from around a pen cap — described by Hackaday as "the smallest Tesla coil secondary you'll ever see" — but the pair ran out of time before the badge-hacking session ended, and the build was never completed. No working device, circuit, or finished badge exists; only the wound coil and Hackaday's contemporaneous writeup and photo document the attempt.
