The 2015 Hackaday SuperConference badge inverted the usual con-badge formula: instead of arriving as a finished gadget, it was handed out as a bare, double-sided FR4 board with a matte black solder mask and a Truchet-tile pattern of traces that led nowhere on purpose. The back carried a grid of through-hole vias at an unusual 0.230" pitch, along with dedicated through-hole and SMT prototyping areas, turning the badge into a literal blank canvas for attendees to solder onto during the conference.

Hackaday leaned into the concept rather than shipping any electronics: no microcontroller, no LEDs, no display, nothing pre-installed. The evening's activities centered on a badge-hacking contest with categories for best "dead bug" build, best blinky mod, and most over-the-top wearable creation — results later recapped in Hackaday's "Best Badges Of The SuperCon" writeup. The organizers even posted the backside Gerbers ahead of the event so people could mill their own copies or plan mods before showing up, though no surviving link to those files was found during this research pass.

No individual designer is credited in any source found; Hackaday's own coverage notes that staff writer Brian Benchoff went on to design the following year's badge, suggesting he was not responsible for this one. The badge was a free give-away to attendees of the November 14-15, 2015 SuperConference at Dogpatch Studios in San Francisco, not sold, and no production quantity is stated anywhere in the available sources.
