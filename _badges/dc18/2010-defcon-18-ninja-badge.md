The 2010 DEF CON 18 Ninja Badge was an unofficial electronic party badge produced by Ninja Networks, the collective behind DEF CON's long-running Ninja Party badges. Built around a 32-bit Freescale MC13224V microcontroller, the badge was pitched by its designers as more than a novelty: a small hackable platform with an SDK available for reprogramming, and a removable display module that could be attached or pulled off the board.

The project was made possible by sponsorship from Facebook and Lookout Mobile Security, and was designed and built by a team split between Boston and Los Angeles: Matt Lewis, Chris Nelson, Amanda Wozniak, and Brandon Creighton. According to a contemporary CSO Online interview, the badge was distributed to DEF CON 18 attendees and made enough of an impression on visiting government, military, and industry figures that Ninja Networks expanded their component order mid-run to keep up with demand.

Specific production numbers, price, and LED/battery details were not published in the sources found, and no hardware or firmware files surfaced during research, so those fields are left empty pending further sourcing.
