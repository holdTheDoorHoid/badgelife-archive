The ShmooCon V (2009) conference badge was a plastic badge cut into a Moose silhouette, designed by G. Mark Hardy to carry that year's badge puzzle. Morse code ran along each long edge of the badge, encoding a 16-character string, while a barcode printed diagonally across the moose's neck both ordered the eight distinct badge variants (one per digit 1-8) and supplied Fibonacci-sequence values used to shift rows of the decoded text. Combining all eight variants and reducing the result with modulo-26 column arithmetic revealed the final answer: "IXNAY ON OOSEMAY," a pig Latin rendering of "nix on noosemay."

The eight badge variants were handed out to different classes of attendees — speakers, security staff, and general attendees among them — so solving the puzzle required attendees to trade or collect badges from people in other roles. The badge is remembered as the puzzle that hooked security researcher David Schuetz ("Darth Null") on ShmooCon's badge-puzzle tradition; he later wrote up the 2009 puzzle's solution and went on to create the ShmooCon 2012 badge puzzle himself, referencing Hardy's 2009 design as the one that "sucked him into a rabbit hole for days."
