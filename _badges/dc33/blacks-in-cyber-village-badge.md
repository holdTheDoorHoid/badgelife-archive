The "Blacks In Cyber Village Badge" entry, as filled in from the maker's own materials, refers to the same item documented elsewhere in this archive as the "BIC Pick": an Afro-pick-shaped badge made by Blacks In Cybersecurity (BiC) for the fifth anniversary of the B.I.C. Village at DEF CON 33 (2025). The maker describes it as a tribute to "our roots and the bold, unapologetic pride" of the community, evoking a pick that has "combed through challenges" alongside its members.

It was sold and traded on-site at the B.I.C. Village during DEF CON 33 rather than through general pre-registration or an online storefront; the maker's page said off-site sales would be "announced in the near future," but no later confirmation of that turned up. Blacks In Cyber also runs a separate Square merchandise store listing generic "PCB 'Blinky' Badges," but nothing on that store ties a distinct product to this village-badge entry, and its product details are not reachable without a JavaScript-capable browser.

No microcontroller, LED, display, or SAO-header specifications were published for this item by the maker, so those fields are left blank rather than guessed. Because the title, maker, event, price, and source page all match the archive's existing `dc33-bic-pick` and `dc33-bic-pick-sao` entries, this is very likely the same physical badge recorded a third time from the community sheet.

## Notes merged from the duplicate entry "BIC Pick"

The BIC Pick is a commemorative badge made by Blacks in Cyber (BiC) for DEF CON 33, marking the five-year anniversary of the BIC Village. It is shaped like an Afro pick, described by the makers as a tribute to "the bold, unapologetic pride that has defined our space since day one." It was made available for purchase and trade on-site at the village during DEF CON 33, with the maker's page noting that off-site sales would be announced separately.

Per the maker's community badge sheet entry, the badge doubles as an SAO: it carries 18 NeoPixel-style LEDs and 6 SAO ports, so it can host other SAOs, plug into a host badge as an SAO itself, or communicate with other badges. It includes CTF and cipher-analysis challenges tied to the official BIC CTF tooling, and part of the badge is meant to be soldered together by the owner as a soldering-skills exercise. None of the hardware specifics in that description (exact MCU, LED part number) could be independently confirmed from the maker's own materials, so the technical fields are left blank pending a source that states them directly.

A YouTube video on the DEF CON conference channel, titled "DEF CON 33 - BiC Village - B I C Pick DEF CON 33 Badge Walkthrough - Eli McRae", covers the badge; its content was not reviewed for this entry.

## Notes merged from the duplicate entry ""BIC PICK" Badge - 2025"

The "BIC PICK" Badge - 2025 is a wearable PCB badge made by Blacks In Cybersecurity (BiC) for the B.I.C. Village's fifth anniversary at DEF CON 33 (2025), custom designed and built by Eli McRae. It is shaped like an Afro pick, which the maker describes as a nod to "the cultural symbol of the Afro pick, an everyday tool that became a statement of pride, identity, and resistance during the Black Power era," carrying that same meaning into hacker culture.

Beyond being a wearable keepsake, the badge lights up and was built to power up to six other add-ons (SAOs), so it can act as a small hub for other people's badge accessories. It can draw power from a connected DEF CON badge from the previous year, or run independently from a USB-C portable charger. The maker's product page jokingly notes the badge is sometimes called an SAO ("Shitty Add On") itself, since it can also plug into another badge that way.

It was sold through Blacks In Cyber's own Square Online store at $35 and tied to the B.I.C. Village; as of this check it is listed as out of stock, with the maker noting boards were "designed and built specifically for 2025," so once sold out they are gone "unless there is major demand." This item is the same physical badge already documented in more general terms under the archive's `dc33-blacks-in-cyber-village-badge` entry.
