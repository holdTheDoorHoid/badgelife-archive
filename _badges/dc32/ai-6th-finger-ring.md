The AI 6th Finger Ring is a novelty accessory made by Seeess (also known for the TOR Lie Detector Badge and TOR Mini Badge at DC32): a flexible, 3D-printed prosthetic finger with a snap-in ring insert, worn to add a convincing extra digit to a hand. The joke is straightforward — since image-generation AI notoriously struggles with hands and fingers, a photo of someone with six fingers can be waved off as "AI generated," giving cover for candid con photos or video.

The design is a remix of an existing Thingiverse prosthetic-finger model, adapted by Seeess to print vertically without supports, fit flush with a slotted ring, and use a separate snap-in insert sized for smaller fingers. It's printed in a pale "fair skin" flexible TPE/TPU filament with no infill so it stays soft and slightly squishy, and the maker notes it can be painted or polished to better match skin tone. There is no electronics, MCU, or SAO interface involved — it's a purely mechanical, wearable 3D print.

The hardware files (finger and insert STLs) and printing notes are published openly on GitHub, making this a fully open-source, replicate-at-home item rather than a limited manufactured badge. The community sheet records a $10 price and a run of 100, but no storefront or maker post confirming those figures at DC32 could be found, so availability is left unknown.
