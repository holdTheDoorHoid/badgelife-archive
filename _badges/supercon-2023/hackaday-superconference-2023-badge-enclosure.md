Tina Belmont (Hackaday.io handle SoftEgg) designed and released this enclosure for the official Hackaday Supercon 2023 badge, known as the Vectorscope badge, ahead of the 2023 Hackaday Superconference. Rather than a single fixed design, the project is meant as a base for the community: the initial "BASIC" version is deliberately "the simplest possible thing that the badge will fit in," giving other attendees a starting point to remix and improve.

The release consists of 57 STL files covering front and back shells, a joystick component, a menu button, and light-pipe elements, ranging from about 108 KB to 5.16 MB each. Files are hosted on both the Hackaday.io project page and a companion GitHub repository. Belmont licensed the designs under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0, explicitly reserving commercial reproduction rights for herself while allowing free printing, modification, and sharing by anyone else.

## Make your own

The hardware files (STLs) are freely available:
1. Download the STL set from the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/softegg/supercon-2023-badge-enclosure) or the [Hackaday.io project page](https://hackaday.io/project/193312-hackaday-superconference-2023-badge-enclosure).
2. 3D print the front/back shell pieces (and optionally the joystick cap, menu button, and light pipe) to fit the Supercon 2023 Vectorscope badge.
3. Remix or modify the design as desired, non-commercially, per the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license.
