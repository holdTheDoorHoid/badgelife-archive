DIPSY is a tiny (10x10mm) breadboard-friendly FPGA development board designed by Antti Lukats, with collaborators Christoph and Andrei Errapart, as an entry to the 2015 Hackaday Prize. It uses a Lattice iCE40UL1K-SWG16 FPGA (1,284 logic cells, 7KB RAM) and was built from just five components for a bill of materials of roughly $1.50, with a target retail price under $5. It offers built-in I2C, onboard LED drivers of varying current capacity, IR transmit/receive, up to 10 I/O pins, and an onboard voltage regulator.

This is a general-purpose electronics prototyping product, not a hacker-conference badge or SAO, and no source ties it to a specific convention or year. Production of the board reportedly hit delays after a theft during the maker's travels; the eventual manufacturing and sales status is not documented in the sources checked. Design files (schematics, PCB layout, and production documentation) are published on GitHub under a folder named for The Hackaday Prize 2015 entry.
