Brian Benchoff, then a writer at Hackaday, made this board as a joke: a "deliberately minimal, artwork-only" PCB meant to needle the increasingly elaborate independent badges that had come to define DEF CON by 2017, while still giving Hackaday its own thing to hand out at DEF CON 25. The two-layer, 70.3 x 50.0 mm board carries no microcontroller, no LEDs, and no SAO header - it does nothing beyond exist as a wearable rectangle of fiberglass, with two quarter-inch holes drilled for a lanyard ("advanced lanyard technology," as the project page puts it). Benchoff worked on it with Jasmine Brackett and Hyr0n, and it was produced in bulk (Hackaday's page says "several thousand" units) through OSH Park and Seeed Studio, offered in a handful of soldermask colors - green, black, red, purple, and gold.

Because the whole point was that anyone could make their own copy of the joke, Benchoff also shared the design as an OSH Park shared project, complete with the bitmap artwork, board file, and Gerbers, so hobbyists could order their own runs long after the con ended.

## Make your own

Gerber files, the EAGLE board file (StupidBadge.brd), and the source bitmap artwork (ArtPositive.bmp, ArtNegative.bmp, ArtNegativeReversed.bmp) are attached to the Hackaday.io project page, and the board can be ordered directly through the OSH Park shared-project link above.
