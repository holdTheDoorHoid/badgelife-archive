The DEF CON 19 (2011) badge marked a deliberate break from the electronics-heavy designs of prior years. Designer Ryan "1o57" Clarke moved the badge to a non-electronic, puzzle-based reality game: each badge is a stack of four 0.040" commercially pure titanium sheets, waterjet-cut and then kiln-oxidized at 1000°F to give the metal an aged, worn look supporting the game's ambience.

Badges were issued across the standard DEF CON attendee categories — Goon, Press, Vendor, Contest, Speaker, Human, and Uber — with each category carrying its own design, and DEF CON's own materials note that the total number of distinct designs actually made was never published. The badge anchored a live reality game that ran through the conference, including a professional actor performing in character and puzzles/Easter eggs pitched at multiple difficulty levels, from straightforward to (in Clarke's words) "how the hell did they figure that out."

No price, exact production quantity, or design-file details were found; the badge was distributed to attendees as part of registration rather than sold. This entry describes the same physical badge as the separately-swept entry `dc19-badge`.
