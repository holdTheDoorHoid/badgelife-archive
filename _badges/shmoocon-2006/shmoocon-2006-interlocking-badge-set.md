The ShmooCon 2006 badge was not a single badge but a set of twenty differently-shaped stainless-steel badges — covering the Speaker, Staff, Shmoo, and Attendee categories — each cut so that, once all twenty were laid out together, they interlocked into one large assembled shape. Completing the set meant either holding one of every category yourself or tracing down other attendees' badges to compare shapes, since no single person was issued more than the one badge type they qualified for.

Per the con's own wiki writeup, attendee Grey Frequency won the (informal) prize for being first to put together a complete interlocking set, after meeting and comparing badges with over 200 other attendees. Community badge-history sheets have since mislabeled Grey Frequency as the badge's maker; the primary source makes clear they were the collector/solver of the puzzle, not its designer or fabricator — no individual credit for the design is given in either surviving source.

The badges carried no electronics; the notable engineering was purely mechanical, in how precisely the twenty stainless steel outlines were profiled to key together. Hackaday's 2006 ShmooCon wrap-up highlighted the set as a memorable piece of that year's swag and pointed to the shmoo wiki, which itself has since gone offline; the page is preserved via the Wayback Machine.
