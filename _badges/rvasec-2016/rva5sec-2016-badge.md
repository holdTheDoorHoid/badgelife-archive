The 2016 RVAsec badge, branded "RVA5sec," was the fifth in HackRVA's annual run of hand-built conference badges, a tradition the Richmond, Virginia hackerspace started in 2011. Per HackRVA's own recap, the badge featured a color screen along with "multiple inputs and sensors," and the boards were made from scratch — etched, populated, and soldered by hand at the hackerspace ahead of the conference. Over 350 boards were built and quality-checked before RVAsec 2016.

Functionally, the badge carried the conference schedule plus games and surprises, continuing HackRVA's practice of layering CTF-style content onto the hardware. Contemporary coverage (via badge.gallery, citing a 2016 interview) describes CTF challenge hooks, standard USB reflashing for attendees to reprogram the badge, and an experimental low-frequency serial channel using the onboard speaker and the badge's own LED sensors — described as "mostly reused from the prior two years with improvements," i.e. building on the RVAs3c (2014) and RVAs4c (2015) designs.

No storefront, price, MCU/LED bill of materials, or public hardware/firmware repository specific to this badge year was found; HackRVA's badges are typically given to attendees rather than sold, and design files for this particular year do not appear to be posted publicly.
