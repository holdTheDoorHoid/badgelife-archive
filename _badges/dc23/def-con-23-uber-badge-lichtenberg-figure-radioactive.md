The DEF CON 23 Uber badge is the "black badge" awarded to the first hackers to solve DEF CON's official on-site puzzle contest in 2015, granting lifetime free admission to the conference. It was designed by L0stBoy (Ryan "1o57" Clarke), the longtime steward of DEF CON's badge and puzzle program, as a tribute to his grandfather, a watchmaker, and to physicist Richard Feynman.

Its base is a triangular acrylic plate scored with Lichtenberg figures — fractal "lightning fossils" burned into the plastic in about 120 nanoseconds by exposing it to a 5-million-volt, 150 kW particle accelerator. Set into the plate's four corners are radioactive and luminescent specimens: a glass marble doped with 3% uranium-238, a tritium vial (rated to glow for roughly 20 years) mounted in a small crystal skull, a sample of trinitite from the Trinity nuclear test site in New Mexico, and raw uranium ore. L0stBoy stated publicly that all the radiation sources were safe to handle, measuring about two orders of magnitude below normal background dose. A coin/medallion was affixed to the badge as part of the puzzle; at least one winner's coin carried a 40-character string on its reverse that became its own small decoding challenge in the community.

Because it was a contest prize rather than a manufactured or sold item, no price, print run, or hardware/firmware files exist for it — only the small number handed to that year's puzzle solvers.
