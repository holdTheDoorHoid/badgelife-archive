The Cockroach Badge is an unofficial DEF CON 26 (2018) badge made by a hardware hacker known as Red (Twitter handle @noidd), covered in Hackaday's "All The Badges Of DEF CON 26 (vol 4)" roundup. It is a large PCB, at least 14 inches long, cut into the shape of a cockroach with spiny, articulated legs branching off the body.

To keep the delicate leg cutouts from snapping off, the maker ran a pair of long antennae parallel to the body on either side of the board, giving the fragile legs extra mechanical support. Hackaday's writer noted they never got a photo of the badge powered up and lit, but the photo used here shows the board already populated: a dense grid of small surface-mount LEDs, unlit, covers most of the body. The site's own intro blurb for the roundup describes the set of badges it covers (this one included) as "a cockroach filled with LEDs," which matches what the photo shows, though no LED count, part number, microcontroller, price, or production quantity is given anywhere in the source, and no dedicated project page, repo, or storefront for it could be found. A second photo in the same article, credited to Twitter, is captioned "Unpopulated PCB" and shows an earlier, bare-board state of the same design.

Because the only confirmed source is a single third-party photo essay rather than the maker's own page, most technical and availability fields are left empty rather than guessed.
