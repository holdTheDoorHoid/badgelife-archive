A wrist-mounted, Pip-Boy-style device spotted at the DEF CON 26 hardware hacking village in 2018 and dubbed the "Brotherhood of Steel" after the Fallout video game faction the Pip-Boy comes from. It packs red bubble-style numeric displays for readouts, four toggle switches, and a d-pad for control, all built into a custom metal case sized to wear on the wrist.

Inside, it runs on an NXP ARM microcontroller powered by a 400 mAh LiPo battery sandwiched between the PCB and the lower half of the case. Also visible with the case open are a laser diode, an Adafruit GPS module, and a Honeywell digital compass, suggesting it was built as a functional multi-tool rather than a purely decorative badge.

No maker, project page, or distribution details turned up in research; this appears to be a one-off hardware-hacking-village build documented only in Hackaday's DEF CON 26 badge roundup, with no evidence it was sold, kitted, or open-sourced.
