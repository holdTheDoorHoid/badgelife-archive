NinjaTel was Ninja Networks' tenth and final DEF CON party badge, issued for DEF CON 20 in Las Vegas (July 26-29, 2012). Rather than a PCB blinky badge, the "badge" was a real, working cellphone: an HTC One V running a customized Android build with a NinjaTel boot logo, one of 650 units handed out to build a private cellular network for the con. Project leads Matt Lewis ("barkode") and Chris Nelson ("cnelson") coordinated the effort, with Eliot Phillips leading construction of the network's mobile hub, a converted van (built with Bob "saberfire" Bristow and Colleen "Phar" Campbell) carrying a USRP software-defined radio, a rooftop antenna, and servers running OpenBTS for the GSM air interface and Asterisk for call routing.

Phones connected over NinjaTel's own network via custom SIM cards, with WiFi/SIP as a fallback, and supported ordinary calls and SMS alongside novelty services: an 800-number collect-call line, conference calling, a "BoozeFone" drink-order line, paging, and easter-egg numbers like 867-5309, with voice prompts recorded by Pat Fleet (AT&T's long-time announcement voice). Physical access to the Ninja party itself used RFID wristbands and engraved shuriken dog tags rather than the phone. The handsets arrived just days before the con, delayed by an Apple-HTC customs dispute at the Port of Los Angeles, and the team charged all 650 units in batches of 96 ahead of distribution. Ninja Networks announced on the DEF CON forums that this would be the group's last Ninja Badge and Event after ten years of Ninja Parties, and the project drew coverage from Ars Technica, The Verge, CNET, Engadget, and The Wall Street Journal.

The NinjaTel van itself was reused the following year to provide wireless connectivity for a 2013 shoot of the CW show "Capture" in a remote location, per Wikipedia's account of the vehicle.
